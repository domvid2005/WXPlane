================================================================================
  WXPlane 5.0 for X-Plane 12  -  User Manual
================================================================================

WXPlane replaces X-Plane 12's weather with live, real-world data: airport
reports (METAR and ATIS), winds and temperatures aloft, clouds, radar storms,
turbulence and windshear. It refreshes every 5 minutes, and whenever you have
flown 40 nm, so the weather keeps up with you along your route.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * X-Plane 12.3 or newer (Windows, macOS or Linux, 64-bit).
  * An internet connection. Without one, the last weather WXPlane applied
    stays in place.
  * No accounts or API keys are needed. Every data source is free and public.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INSTALLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Close X-Plane.
  2. Copy the whole "WXPlane" folder (the one that contains win_x64,
     mac_x64 and lin_x64) into:

         X-Plane 12/Resources/plugins/

     You should end up with  X-Plane 12/Resources/plugins/WXPlane/win_x64/
     WXPlane.xpl  (and the same for the other platforms).
  3. If you have an older WXPlane, delete its folder first. If you have the
     old "WeatherFix" plugin, delete that too.
  4. Start X-Plane.
  5. IMPORTANT: turn OFF X-Plane's own real-world weather (X-Plane's
     Weather settings: choose manual weather rather than real weather).
     If you leave it on, X-Plane keeps downloading its own weather and mixes
     it with WXPlane's. While it is on, WXPlane's window shows a red warning
     and every control is locked until you turn it off.

  Updates: WXPlane works with SkunkCrafts Updater. Its folder carries a
  skunkcrafts_updater.cfg, so the updater app lists WXPlane with your other
  add-ons and downloads only the files that changed. Your settings
  (WXPlane.cfg in Output/preferences) are never touched by an update.

  macOS: if macOS blocks the plugin the first time, allow it in
  System Settings > Privacy & Security, then restart X-Plane.

  Uninstall: delete the WXPlane folder from Resources/plugins. You can also
  delete WXPlane.cfg from X-Plane 12/Output/preferences.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. OPENING THE WEATHER WINDOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Menu: Plugins > WXPlane opens the window. Choose it again (or close the
    window) to hide it.
  * Key or joystick button: in X-Plane's Settings > Keyboard (or Joystick),
    search for "WXPlane" and bind:
        "WXPlane: open/close the weather window (GUI)"  (wxplane/weather_window)
        "WXPlane: refresh the weather now"              (wxplane/refresh)
  * At the top right of the window:
        Refresh  - downloads fresh data now. X-Plane fades the new weather in
                   over 1-2 minutes, so the sim does not stutter.
        Pop out  - moves the window into its own OS window (for a second
                   monitor), and back again.
  * The window follows X-Plane's UI scale and also works in VR.
  * The header shows when the next update is due and when the last one
    finished (in UTC).
  * "Loading weather m:ss" (amber, with a turning ring) counts down while
    the weather is built from scratch: at start-up, when WXPlane is
    switched back on, and after a jump to a new place (a new flight or a
    teleport). Until it ends, the sky may not match the reports yet.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE TABS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
  Everything at a glance, without scrolling:
  * The nearest airport: its raw METAR, decoded wind, visibility, weather,
    pressure, temperature, ceiling and flight category (VFR, MVFR, IFR,
    LIFR).
  * The wind dial. Click it to switch between:
        North Lock            - north is up.
        Airplane Heading Lock - your heading is up. The centre then shows
                                the headwind or tailwind (H/T) and the
                                crosswind (X, from the left or right).
    The map turns with the dial, and the turn is smooth. The small box under
    the dial says which: North Lock or Heading Lock.
  * Nearby airports. Click a column header to sort (see AIRPORTS below).
  * A small map that shows the same layers you chose on the Map tab.
  * Winds and temperatures aloft, with the tropopause.
  * Turbulence and windshear at your aircraft. The windshear risk is
    coloured from white (none) to red (high).

MAP
  A larger map around your aircraft.
  * Zoom with the mouse wheel or the - / + buttons: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40, 80,
    160 and 320 nm. The zoom eases smoothly between steps.
  * Drag to pan. "Center on aircraft" glides back to you. While the map is
    moved off the aircraft a small dark + marks its centre, and the LOCAL
    time bubble shows the local time at the +.
  * North Lock / Airplane Heading Lock button (the same as clicking the
    wind dial).
  * Layer buttons:
        Radar     - real radar echoes, coloured by strength (US and Europe).
        Map       - coastlines, borders, state/province lines and lakes.
                    The map is built in, so nothing is downloaded for it.
        Airports  - stations coloured by flight category. Click one to see
                    its METAR and ATIS.
        Turb      - turbulence on a 12 nm grid, at the altitude slider's
                    level.
        Winds     - wind barbs at the slider's level (NOAA winds aloft
                    stations in white, GFS model points in blue). Click a
                    barb to decode it: direction, speed, what each barb
                    and pennant means, temperature, ISA deviation, and the
                    headwind and crosswind on your heading.
        Clouds    - the clouds WXPlane gave X-Plane at the slider's level,
                    outlined. Thin layers are light grey; the deeper the
                    layer, the darker the grey.
        Time Zone - the time zone boundaries as dashed violet lines
                    (Natural Earth time zones).
        PIREPs    - pilot reports as triangles: urgent (UUA) red, routine
                    (UA) orange. Click one for its time, aircraft, level,
                    turbulence, icing, sky, weather, temperature, wind and
                    the full text.
        AIRMET    - US G-AIRMETs, coloured by hazard: IFR purple, mountain
                    obscuration pink, turbulence yellow, icing light blue,
                    surface wind green, low-level windshear rose.
        SIGMET    - SIGMETs worldwide and US convective SIGMETs: convective
                    red, turbulence orange, icing blue, volcanic ash brown,
                    tropical cyclone magenta.
    Click inside an AIRMET or SIGMET area to see its hazard, levels,
    validity, movement and text. Airports and pilot reports inside an area
    can still be clicked.
  * Altitude slider (right of the map): sets the level for Turb, Winds
    and Clouds, from the surface to FL600. It is greyed out while none of
    those three layers is on.
        ACFT - the slider follows your aircraft as it climbs and descends.
        SFC  - the surface: the slider goes to the bottom ("SFC"), Winds
               shows the airports' reported winds and Clouds the lowest
               layer.
        COMP - composite, all altitudes at once: every cloud layer
               together (darker where the column holds more cloud), the
               most turbulent level at each point, and the strongest wind
               at each point with its flight level (its decode lists every
               level).
    Click ACFT, SFC or COMP again to go back to the altitude you chose
    with the slider. Dragging the slider ends them. The slider clicks into
    whole thousands of feet when it comes within 300 ft of one, harder
    into every 5,000 ft and hardest into every 10,000 ft (which light
    amber when the slider sits on them); the mouse wheel steps 1,000 ft. Max turbulence clicks into
    tenths the same way.
  * Radar echoes are drawn as a radar picture: green edges into yellow,
    orange and red cores.
  * True and magnetic: winds are given in degrees true (°T) as METARs and
    winds aloft report them, with the other reference in a small box next
    to them. On an airport's details (and its map popup), click that box
    to swap true and magnetic. The °T / °M switch above the airport lists
    sets the lists (Overview and Airports tab together). ATIS winds are
    magnetic; METAR winds are true.
  * Popups can be moved: drag a popup by its background. Two can be open
    at once and they never touch.
  * "TIME" (Zulu) is at the top left of the map; "Center on aircraft" and
    North Lock / Heading Lock are at its top right.
  * Click the circle of an airport for its METAR, ATIS and surface wind,
    and a wind barb's staff or feathers for its decode (the selected barb
    turns amber). Barbs are blue, deeper the stronger the wind; a calm
    wind is a white circle.
  * Zulu and local time: in a box under the airport on the Overview, in
    every airport's details, and at the top left of the Map tab's map.
    Local times carry their time zone's name (EST, CDT, GMT, CEST ...) in
    North America and Europe, and the UTC offset elsewhere; "~" marks a
    time estimated from the longitude (far from the aircraft).
  * The Airports tab also shows today's sunrise and sunset at the
    airport (local time and Zulu).
  * The map title shows the radar region: "Map - US" (NOAA MRMS) or
    "Map - Europe" (EUMETNET OPERA). There is no radar elsewhere.
  * The list next to the map shows the strongest storm cells (strength,
    rain rate, top, distance and bearing).

AIRPORTS
  * Search: type any ICAO code in the world (for example EGLL, KJFK, RJTT),
    or a US field's 3-character FAA code without the K (35D, 1B9, BOS),
    to see its current METAR and, where one exists, its ATIS. This is for
    reading only. It does not change the weather in the sim.
  * Below the search box: every station WXPlane is using, with category,
    wind, visibility, weather, ceiling and temperature.
  * Sorting: click a column header once to sort one way, again for the
    other way, and a third time to go back to nearest-first. Equal values
    are listed nearest first.
  * Select a station to see its report, its ATIS, and exactly what WXPlane
    sent to X-Plane for it.

WINDS & TEMPS
  The wind and temperature profile above your aircraft, every 1,000 to
  2,000 ft up to FL600, with the deviation from standard temperature (ISA)
  and the tropopause.

TURBULENCE
  * Turbulence (EDR) by altitude at your aircraft, and where it comes from.
  * Max turbulence - scales all of WXPlane's turbulence from 0 (none) to
    1.0 (as strong as the data says).
  * Every nearby pilot report. Click one to open its details below it, and
    click again to close it.
  * Windshear near you.
  * Live at the aircraft: the EDR WXPlane is giving X-Plane right now (after
    Max turbulence) on a bar over the intensity bands, and a g meter of the
    load factor the aircraft feels (the last 30 s, and its range over the
    last minute). Pilot reports are on the Overview and the Map tab.
  * Windshear odds (0-1.0): how often gust fronts and microbursts form.
    1.0 is the realistic rate; 0 means none at all (like Max turbulence 0).
  * Another turbulence add-on running (ActiveSky XP, xProTurb, the X-Crafts
    ERJ's EICAS plugin,
    XPRealistic ...): WXPlane leaves X-Plane's turbulence to it. A red
    TURBULENCE CONFLICT caution covers this tab and its controls are
    locked until that add-on is disabled. The switch in Settings still
    works (to turn WXPlane's turbulence and windshear off altogether).

SERVICES
  Every data source WXPlane uses, with its state:
      OK        (green)  working.
      DEGRADED  (amber)  the last download failed. Older data is in use.
      DOWN      (red)    not working. It also shows when it last worked.
      grey               not used in this region, off, or waiting.
  The tab title shows how many services have a problem, for example
  "Services (1)". The Radar section below lists both radar sources, NOAA
  MRMS (US) and EUMETNET OPERA (Europe), and says which one is not used
  because the aircraft is outside its coverage.

SETTINGS
  * WXPlane weather on - turns all of WXPlane's weather on or off. When it
    is off, the weather fades back to X-Plane's own, and the other settings
    are greyed out.
  * Use ATIS - merge the digital ATIS (major US airports) over the METAR.
  (GFS winds and temperatures aloft, clouds above what the airports
  report and the weather over open water are always on.)
  * Realistic turbulence - WXPlane sets the turbulence at every altitude
    from real data (NOAA GTGN in the US, an ECMWF-based estimate
    elsewhere, and pilot reports), instead of X-Plane's own.
  * Realistic windshear - gust fronts and microburst outflow near real
    storms.
  (Max turbulence, which scales all of WXPlane's turbulence from 0 to 1.0,
  is on the Turbulence tab.)
  * Keyboard - how to bind the window to a key (see section 3).
  * Data & credits.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WXPLANE DOES TO THE WEATHER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Airports: the nearest 40 reports within 120 nm set the surface wind,
    visibility, fog, mist, clouds, precipitation, temperature and pressure.
    The weather is blended smoothly between airports, with no gaps.
  * ATIS: for the two nearest airports within 35 nm, the D-ATIS values
    replace the METAR's (US only).
  * Aloft: GFS winds and temperatures from the surface to FL600, and GFS
    clouds above what the airports can see, such as cirrus.
  * Open water: away from airports, the GFS model provides the weather.
  * Radar storms: over the US (NOAA MRMS) and Europe (EUMETNET OPERA), real
    radar echoes become rain, showers and thunderstorms in the right
    places, with realistic tops. Updated every 5 minutes.
  * Turbulence: clear-air and storm turbulence at every altitude from real
    data and pilot reports.
  * Windshear: gust fronts and microbursts around real convective storms.
  * Runways: dry runways when there has been no recent rain.
  * Updates fade in over 1-2 minutes, so there are no sudden jumps or
    freezes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ADVANCED SETTINGS (WXPlane.cfg)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Advanced settings are in  X-Plane 12/Output/preferences/WXPlane.cfg,
  which is created on first start. Edit it with X-Plane closed. Useful keys
  (the default is in brackets):

      update_minutes            (5)    minutes between updates
      search_nm                 (120)  how far to look for airport reports
      max_stations              (40)   how many reports to apply
      refetch_move_nm           (40)   update early after flying this far
      atis_range_nm             (35)   ATIS search range
      radar_range_nm            (150)  how far radar storms are built
      max_turbulence            (1)    the Max turbulence slider
      load_fade_s               (60)   how long the loading countdown
                                       allows X-Plane to fade new weather in
      manual_refresh_immediate  (0)    1 = Refresh applies at once, with
                                       a short pause, instead of fading in

  The README in the source repository lists every key. If something goes
  wrong, delete WXPlane.cfg and WXPlane writes a fresh one with the
  defaults.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The window has a red warning and nothing can be clicked
      X-Plane's own real-world weather is on. Turn it off in X-Plane's
      Weather settings.

  WXPlane is not in the Plugins menu
      Check the folder layout in section 2. The .xpl file must be in
      Resources/plugins/WXPlane/<platform>_x64/. Look in X-Plane 12/Log.txt
      for lines starting with [WXPlane], or for an error loading the
      plugin.

  The weather looks old, or there is no radar
      Open the Services tab. A red or amber service is having problems,
      and the tab shows when it last worked. The European radar feed
      (OPERA) sometimes stops for a few hours. WXPlane then tries
      EUMETNET's backup store as well; if neither has a picture, the
      Services tab says how old the newest one is. There is no radar outside the US and
      Europe. There, storms come from the airport reports and the GFS
      model.

  No ATIS
      D-ATIS covers major US airports only.

  Reporting a problem
      Please include X-Plane 12/Log.txt from the flight. Every WXPlane line
      starts with [WXPlane] and records what was downloaded and what was
      sent to X-Plane.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. KNOWN LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * X-Plane's own weather radar instrument draws from the sim's clouds and
    rain. WXPlane makes those match real radar, but it cannot feed radar
    images to the instrument directly.
  * The winds aloft, clouds aloft and open-water weather come from a model
    forecast (GFS, about 25 km). They are used only where no report covers
    the spot.
  * Laminar Research marks X-Plane's weather API as experimental.
